> The theme library method: 6 themes, each with a STAR story and a trade-off. Reframing hardware/embedded stories for AI leadership signals.

## Industry context (2025–2026)

- **What Staff+ Physical AI loops test for:** Technical vision (roadmap across HW/SW), cross-functional leadership (working across algorithm, hardware, systems, and product teams), decision-making under ambiguity (AI for hardware = high uncertainty), quantifiable impact (silicon area reduction, latency improvement, fleet-wide success rate)
- **Companies and their behavioral signals:**
  - Google/Pixel: "Googleyness" → collaboration, intellectual humility, user-centric thinking
  - Apple: "functional expertise" → deep technical ownership, attention to craft, working within secrecy constraints
  - Qualcomm: "technical leadership" → cross-team influence, customer-facing technical decisions, roadmap ownership
  - NVIDIA: "technical depth" → low-level systems expertise, performance optimization, CUDA/GPU mastery
  - Robotics startups (Physical Intelligence, Figure AI, 1X): "builder mentality" → speed, pragmatism, willingness to do everything from research to deployment
  - Meta: "move fast" → bias toward action, data-driven disagreement, cross-team impact
- **The theme library method:** Prepare 6 stories, each mapped to a theme. Any behavioral question maps to one or more themes. Practice the mapping, not the story.

## What the loops actually ask

- Tell me about the most technically impactful project you led. What was the result? (Theme: Technical Roadmap)
- Tell me about a time you proposed an unconventional technical approach that others initially resisted. (Theme: HW/SW Co-Design Innovation)
- Tell me about a cross-functional project where the teams had different priorities. How did you align them? (Theme: XFN Sensor-Algorithm Collaboration)
- Tell me about a technical disagreement with a senior colleague. How did you resolve it? (Theme: Data-Driven Disagreement)
- Tell me about a project with high ambiguity — you didn't know if the approach would work. (Theme: Debugging Ambiguity in AI for Hardware)
- Tell me about how you raised the engineering bar on your team. (Theme: Engineering Process and Mentorship)
- Tell me about a failure or a mistake. What did you learn?
- How do you set technical roadmaps for a team of 5–10 engineers?
- How do you decide when to stop iterating and ship?
- How do you communicate technical risk to non-technical stakeholders?

## The 6 themes

| # | Theme | Physical AI signal | Key metric |
|---|-------|-------------------|------------|
| 1 | Technical Roadmap | You set the multi-generation technical direction across HW and SW | Shipped across N generations |
| 2 | HW/SW Co-Design Innovation | You changed the algorithm to make the hardware cheaper/faster | % area/power/latency reduction |
| 3 | XFN Sensor-Algorithm Collaboration | You aligned sensor, algorithm, and systems teams | Cross-team launch with measurable quality |
| 4 | Data-Driven Disagreement | You used data to resolve a technical disagreement | Decision reversed + outcome |
| 5 | Debugging Ambiguity | You navigated high uncertainty in AI applied to hardware | Root-caused and shipped despite ambiguity |
| 6 | Engineering Process & Mentorship | You raised the bar for the team | Engineers promoted, process adopted org-wide |
